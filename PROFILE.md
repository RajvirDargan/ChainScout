# Candidate profile — example (Alex Chen)

Source of truth for scoring. This is a **fictional example profile** shipped so the
repo is runnable and demonstrates the full rubric end to end — swap it for your own
background before running this for real. Built by hand for illustration; a real
profile would typically be distilled from a CV plus a longer personal dossier.

---

## 1. Hard constraints — check these first

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Nationality | Example: non-EU |
| Current permit | Example: post-study orientation permit, **expires in ~6 months** — every role below therefore needs sponsorship |
| Degree | **BSc only** (example: State University, Industrial Engineering). Rules out any permit gated on a master's. |
| Experience | ~1–1.5 years post-graduation, much of it internship-titled. Rules out permits gated on 3+ years. |
| Languages | English (working). Local language A1–A2. **No other languages.** Needs English-working roles everywhere. |
| Availability | Immediate / within 2 weeks — a genuine selling point |
| Current location | Example: Rotterdam, NL — relocating |
| Salary floor (NL) | **≥ €3,122/month gross, excluding 8% holiday allowance** — 2026 IND reduced recent-graduate criterion (a public statutory figure — check the current one before relying on it). An employer below this **cannot** sponsor at any price. |
| Geography priority | **1) Netherlands** 2) Germany 3) Belgium 4) Ireland 5) Poland 6) Dubai / Middle East 7) home country |

**The Netherlands remains first priority and the preferred outcome.** It gets the full
title list, a guaranteed floor of slots in the email, and its own top section in the
digest. Before the permit expiry any Dutch employer can hire with no sponsorship;
after that the employer must be an IND-recognised sponsor (*erkend referent*) filing a
kennismigrant permit.

The countries below NL widen the net, ordered by how viable the work permit actually
is — not by market size.

Two advantages worth weighting in a candidate's favour if applicable, both often
**time-limited**:

- An EU-recognised degree skips the foreign-degree recognition bottleneck in some
  countries (e.g. Germany's anabin/ZAB process).
- Age- or recency-bounded thresholds (a post-graduation Blue Card discount window, an
  under-30 rate, a "graduated within N years" rule) mean the candidate's position on
  paper is often better *now* than it will be in a few years — worth weighing against
  "build experience first, move later".

## 5. Scoring rubric — 0–100

| Dimension | Max | How to award |
|---|---|---|
| **Role fit** | 40 | 34–40 core title from §4. 24–33 strong adjacent. 12–23 adjacent/lower. 0–11 not a fit. Weight the *content* of the posting over the title. |
| **Seniority fit** | 15 | 13–15 graduate / junior / 0–2 yrs / traineeship. 8–12 asks 2–3 yrs. 3–7 asks 3–5 yrs. 0–2 senior, lead, manager, 5+ yrs. |
| **Language** | 15 | 13–15 English-working or explicitly international team. 7–12 local language "a plus" / bilingual welcome. 0–5 fluent local language required. A candidate with no other languages should score a stated fluency requirement as close to disqualifying, not a minor deduction. |
| **Visa viability** | 20 | Per country — see the table below. Never score a job down merely because a sponsor lookup failed: use the mid band and flag it as unknown. |
| **Location** | 10 | **NL:** 9–10 base city / hybrid-remote. 6–8 Randstad (Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Utrecht, Den Haag). 3–5 elsewhere in NL. **DE:** 9–10 major hub or hybrid-remote. 6–8 elsewhere. **BE:** 9–10 Antwerp/Brussels. 6–8 elsewhere. **IE:** 8–10 Dublin, Cork. 5–7 elsewhere. **PL:** 8–10 the English-language SSC belt (Kraków, Wrocław, Gdańsk, Warsaw). 4–6 elsewhere. **Dubai/ME:** 6–8. **Home country:** 5–7 major metro, 2–4 elsewhere. |

### Visa viability by country (20 pts)

| Country | 16–20 | 10–15 | 0–9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Netherlands** | On the IND recognised-sponsor register **and** stated or implied salary at/above the statutory floor | Large multinational, sponsor status unknown | Salary stated below the floor, or "no sponsorship / must have EU work rights". Never score down merely because the register lookup failed — use 10–15 and flag it unknown |
| **Germany** | English-working role at a firm that hires internationally, pay meets a reasonable bar, or sponsorship/relocation named outright | Large multinational, sponsorship not mentioned | "No sponsorship" / "must have EU work rights" stated, or German fluency required |
| **Belgium** | Pay meets the under-30 statutory rate **and** Flanders-based, or sponsorship named | Multinational, pay unstated | Pay below the under-30 rate, or a junior salary elsewhere |
| **Ireland** | Sponsorship explicitly offered, or the employer is a known permit sponsor | Multinational, sponsorship unstated — **cap at 15**, see below | "EU work rights required", or an agency posting |
| **Poland** | Shared-service centre or multinational running in English, sponsorship plausible | Local employer, sponsorship unstated | Local-language fluency required |
| **Dubai & home region** | Sponsorship mentioned, or the employer is a multinational | Otherwise | Nationality-restricted (prescreen-rejected anyway) |

**Two country-specific rules that override the generic bands (illustrative — verify
current rules for your own situation):**

1. **Ireland caps at 15 unless sponsorship is explicit.** A permit may be legally open
   to the candidate, but it can still require a labour market test that employers
   routinely refuse to run for junior hires. Legal eligibility is not sponsorship
   willingness — score the willingness.
2. **Germany: don't penalise pay below an upgrade-path threshold if the base route has
   no statutory minimum for the applicant's situation.** A below-threshold salary can
   still be fully viable and should score in the top band on everything but pay.

**Bands:** ≥70 Apply now · 55–69 Worth a look · 45–54 Watchlist · <45 store silently,
never email.

**Tie-break:** the digest splits into two top-level sections — **Netherlands** first,
then **Beyond the Netherlands** (in permit-route viability order).

Selection into the email is **by score alone, with one exception**: the priority
country holds a guaranteed floor of slots (`min_priority_geo_slots`) so it never has
to out-score the rest of the world for every place. Everything above that floor is
still won on score. That split matters — making the *whole* selection priority-country-
first once let a low-scoring local role crowd out a much higher-scoring role elsewhere,
which is the failure this rule was written for.
